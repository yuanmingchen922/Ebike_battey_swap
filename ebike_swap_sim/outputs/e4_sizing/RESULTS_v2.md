## Interpretation

### What changed with the fixed baseline (both regimes; ILP rows untouched)

1. **Byte-identity and backward compatibility hold.** Every regenerated variant has the old `n_arcs` and `q0_mean`, and the `baseline_legacy` variant (`jit_dispatch = False`, `swap_on_reach_refusal = False`) reproduces the old baseline total to 1e-6 on all 78 variants (51 main + 27 stressed). The ILP rows are therefore comparable as they stand; ILP <= new baseline on every variant.
2. **Main regime (q0 ~ U{0.4L..L}): the fixed baseline is uniformly better, and the sizing conclusions do not move.** Cost 949.3 -> 885.4 at the default sizes (-6.7 %), unserved 4.67 -> 4.00, bike km 108.1 -> 103.2; the fixed baseline is at most as expensive as the old one on all 51 variants. The whole change is the just-in-time dispatch rule (F1): the ablation table shows `jit_only` = `v2` on every size (11.7 JIT dispatches per run), while the dead-zone rule (F2) never fires here (`reach_refusals` 0 once JIT is on; the legacy policy had 0.33 reach-only refusals per run; `deadzone_only` alone is +4 at B_j = 1 and -55 to -56 at B_j >= 2 against legacy, but adds nothing once JIT is on). The headline gap at B_j = 4, C_j = 3, P_j = 4 kW falls from 40.9 % to **36.1 %** of the baseline cost (per seed 44.4 / 20.4 / 43.5 %; 60.1 % of the ILP cost; `baseline_reserve` 35.9 %), with the unserved component still the whole gap (333 of 332 units: 4.00 vs 0.67 orders at P_i = 100; response -18.7, lateness +10.0, travel +4.6, electricity +3.0). Marginal values are unchanged in sign and size: a spare battery is worth -0.25 / -0.05 / 0 / 0 (operational, per unit at one site, B 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4 -> 6) for the fixed baseline, i.e. nothing beyond B_j = 2 as for the ILP (0.70 / 0.07 / 0 / 0); a charger -0.23 / -0.05 / 0 and a kW -0.34 / -0.07 / 0 (ASAP charging only buys electricity); the B x C grid stays a pure setup-cost plane (operational 770.6-771.5 for the fixed baseline, 439.2-439.4 for the ILP). The baseline's 4.00 unserved orders are identical at every B_j, C_j, P_j: the service level of the ILP (0.67 = the two orders no bike can serve) is not reachable by sizing, so the 'extra spares to match the ILP service level' statement stays "not within the sweep" for every baseline variant (the plan's "about one more battery per site" is wrong in this regime for the same reason as before: the gap is a dispatch effect, not a stock effect).
3. **Swap-stressed regime (q0 ~ U{3..6}): the fixed baseline is worse at every B_j <= 4, better only at B_j = 6, and the sizing statements for the baseline move in the pessimistic direction.** Cost 978.8 -> 1060.6 at the default sizes (+8.4 %), unserved 4.33 -> 5.33, headline gap 40.7 % -> **44.8 %** (per seed 40.5 / 38.1 / 55.8 %); over the 27 stressed variants the fixed baseline is at most as expensive as the old one on only 3. The B sweep: 1924.8 / 1501.8 / 1230.3 / 1060.6 / 951.2 (fixed) vs 1725.1 / 1343.6 / 1065.0 / 978.8 / 989.9 (old) at B_j = 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 6, unserved 15.7 / 10.3 / 7.3 / 5.3 / 4.0 vs 13.3 / 8.7 / 5.3 / 4.3 / 4.3. The marginal value of a spare is now 144 / 93 / 60 / 21 per unit (operational; old 130 / 96 / 32 / 1.1), i.e. the knee where a battery stops paying for bB = 3 lies beyond B_j = 6 for the fixed baseline (it was at B_j = 4 for the old one, and at B_j = 2 for the ILP: 61.9 / 0.96 / 0.72 / 0.34). In the C sweep the fixed baseline keeps improving up to C_j = 3 (1194 / 1137 / 1061 / 1067; old 988 / 971 / 979 / 985) with a charger worth 21 / 27.5 / -0.2 per unit (old 7.6 / -0.6 / -0.1), so "one charger per site suffices for the ILP, the baseline wants two" becomes "the baseline wants three". The old statement "the baseline needs B_j = 4 to get where the ILP already is at B_j = 2" becomes "> 6" for the fixed baseline; measured by service level neither baseline ever reaches the ILP's 0.67 unserved within the sweep (fixed 4.0, old 4.33 at B_j = 6).
4. **Why the fix hurts under swap stress (ablation, `ablation_fixes_v2.csv`).** Both new rules are individually harmful at the default stock: `jit_only` 1033.6 vs legacy 978.8 at B_j = 4 (1107 vs 1065 at B_j = 3) and `deadzone_only` is worse at B_j <= 2 (1889.7 vs 1725.1 at B_j = 1; 1421.9 vs 1343.6 at B_j = 2) and only better at B_j >= 4 (971.1 vs 978.8; 911.3 vs 989.9 at B_j = 6); their combination (`v2`) is worst at B_j = 1..4. Mechanism: with low initial charge most bikes are low on energy and the swap sites are the bottleneck (stock-out slots 5.7 -> 11.0 at B_j = 4, swaps 10 per run either way). The JIT rule commits the *nearest* bike to an order whose food is not ready and parks it (wait arcs) until a_i - tau_r; in the legacy policy that bike stayed idle and dispatchable and the order was picked up later by a bike that was free and charged at a_i, so legacy effectively postponed the assignment decision to a boundary with more information. The dead-zone rule sends every bike that fails the post-delivery reach guard (q <= max_need) to swap at once; with 1-2 spares per site this empties the sites early (stock-out slots 5.7 -> 11.0 at B_j = 4) and the queued bikes serve nothing while the next full battery charges. Both rules remove the two *library* asymmetries the reviewer found (dominance-ON and dominance-OFF libraries now give the same schedule), but they are greedy rules, not improvements of the policy: the fix is a different naive policy, better when energy is slack (main regime, E2: -1.4 % cost, -5.9 % km, -0.2 unserved; E4 main: -6.7 %) and worse when energy is scarce (E4 stressed: +8.4 %; E3 separated layouts: +5.4 % under NONE).
5. **MIP-start acceptance.** The new baseline start is accepted by the written model on 34 / 51 main variants: every variant with B_j >= 4 (the whole C and P sweeps, the B_j = 4 / 6 grid rows) and B_j = 3 on seed 1; rejected are B_j = 1 and B_j = 2 (every seed and C_j), B_j = 3 on seeds 0 and 2 and the `free_fixed` sizes (one spare per site), always by (17) (R_end = 1 at a site the policy empties) and mostly also by (18). In the stressed regime 18 / 27 are accepted; rejected are B_j = 1, C_j = 1 (B4_C1_P4) and `free_fixed` on every seed. Removing (18) alone changes nothing (the (17) rows are the binding ones); under the fully relaxed end targets (rho = 0, R_end = 0) every one of the 78 starts is accepted (Gurobi fix-and-solve, 7 s build + 7 s of fixed solves per variant, objective equal to `evaluate.cost` on every accepted start, certificate equal to `evaluate.check` on all runs). For comparison the old baseline start was accepted by only 10 of the 24 written-model ILP runs that used it in the main regime (seed 0 and seed 2 reported (17)/(18) at B_j = 4; the other 27 runs started from the `ILP_rho0` chain, accepted 13 / 27) and by 6 / 6 in the stressed regime (ILP_rho0 chain 10 / 21); the fixed policy ends with more full spares at the sites in the main regime (`end_full_total` 7.0 -> 7.3 at B_j = 4, and no site is left without its R_end = 1 spare on seeds 0 and 2, where the old policy reported (17) at 1-3 sites).
6. **Recommendation.** Report both baselines: `baseline_legacy` is the deck's naive policy as originally implemented, `baseline` (fixed) is the library-invariant version; the headline gaps are 36.1 % (fixed) vs 40.9 % (old) in the main regime and 44.8 % vs 40.7 % in the stressed regime, so the coordinator's advantage is 36-45 % of the baseline cost whichever naive policy is used, and the sizing conclusions for the ILP (knee at two spares per site, one charger per site, power never binding) are untouched. If a single baseline is wanted, the natural remedy for item 4 is to let the JIT rule compete with the "dispatch at a_i" alternative (postpone when another bike will be free and closer at a_i) and to cap the dead-zone rule by the site's expected full stock; both are policy design choices for the supervisor, not bugs.
