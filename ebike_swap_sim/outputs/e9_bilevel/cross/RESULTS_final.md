# E9.5 (final) -- cross-evaluation of the chosen layouts under the ILP and the baseline policy

Family `nyc4h_stress2` (6 bikes, 36 orders, `q0 ~ U{3..6}` of 12 slots, `P_j = 2.8 kW`, 4 kW group); 12 Midtown candidates (`candidates.csv`); seeds 0-2 were the search seeds (in-sample), seeds 3-4 are out-of-sample; every scenario has weight 1/5. Lower level: E2 ILP with facilities fixed, `siting_pruning(targets='open_sites')`, Gurobi TimeLimit 300 s, Threads 3, Seed 0; baseline = the R2.3 myopic policy on the same instance and arc library ((9)/(17)/(18) reported, not enforced). Written model `rho = 0.5`, `R_end = 1` at swap-capable objects; the relaxed control sets `rho = R_end = 0`. `V = C_setup + mean_s OPEX_s` in USD per 4 h shift (allocated setup cost `C_setup` = investment amortised to the shift, 12.78-53.43 USD). Every ILP total is an upper bound on the written optimum (moves only into candidate / depot nodes, E8 convention); the R2.4 geometry caveat applies (real restaurants, zone weights and candidate coordinates on a synthetic 150 m grid; elevation and signals synthetic). Source run: `logs/cross.log`, `cross/records.jsonl` (7 `cross` rows + 66 fresh `lower_level` rows), finished 2026-10-14 21:49 after 55.8 min; in-sample lower-level rows that were cache hits in this run are taken from `sweep/` and `deck/` records (same cache version `e9-2`, same `open_sites` libraries).

## Summary

The seven layouts that the search algorithms chose (greedy + local search at every budget and role set, the deck heuristic at m = 1..4, and the restricted-menu (A)/(B) validation layout) were re-priced on five demand scenarios under the lower-level ILP and under the baseline policy. Under the ILP the three single-SC-site layouts with at least three spares (`L02:SC:B3:C2`, `L02:SC:B4:C2`, `L01:SC:B3:C2`) are indistinguishable (V = 654.49-654.65 USD, regret <= 0.16 USD), the multi-site deck layouts lose exactly their extra setup cost (regret 12.4 / 25.4 / 38.6 USD at 2 / 3 / 4 sites against an operating saving of at most 1.9 USD), and the cheaper `L02:SC:B2:C1` loses 8.3 USD mainly because one more order per shift goes unserved. Under the baseline policy the spread is four times larger (regret 0-150.6 USD): the policy wants the fourth spare (`L02:SC:B4:C2` is best, 47.4 USD better than the ILP-best `L02:SC:B3:C2`) and saves 48-67 USD of operating cost with the deck's 2-4 site layouts relative to the single three-spare site, of which 48 USD is obtained by the fourth spare alone, so the extra sites are at best break-even against their 13-40 USD of setup, while the two-spare layout costs it 150.6 USD. The ILP beats the policy by 31.1-41.3 % of the policy's cost on every layout (lateness 5-7 USD vs 188-254 USD, served share 0.83-0.86 vs 0.71-0.85), and the gap is smallest on the layouts with the most sites because only the policy benefits from them. The siting decision itself is robust to the operating policy (every method and both policies put the single site at L02, and L01 is equivalent within 4 USD), but the sizing is not: the policy's best size is one spare larger than the coordinator's, and the policy's regret for taking the coordinator's layout (47 USD, 4.8 %) is 300 times the coordinator's regret for taking the policy's (0.15 USD, 0.02 %).

## Best layout under each policy; is the siting robust to the operating policy?

* Best under the ILP: `L02:SC:B3:C2` (V = 654.49 USD, chosen by greedy + LS at m = 1); `L02:SC:B4:C2` and `L01:SC:B3:C2` are 0.15 and 0.16 USD behind, i.e. tied within the solver's tolerance.
* Best under the baseline policy: `L02:SC:B4:C2` (V = 978.87 USD, the layout greedy + LS chose at m = 2 and 3 under every role set); the ILP-best `L02:SC:B3:C2` is 47.4 USD (4.8 %) worse for the policy, the deck's m = 1 layout `L01:SC:B3:C2` 43.4 USD worse, and the restricted-menu layout `L02:SC:B2:C1` 150.6 USD (15.4 %) worse.
* Cross-regret: taking the policy-best layout under the ILP costs 0.15 USD (0.02 %); taking the ILP-best layout under the policy costs 47.4 USD (4.8 % of the policy's optimum). The asymmetry anticipated from E8 (the ILP absorbs siting, the policy does not) survives on the stressed family.
* Location: robust. Every search method, both budgets that matter and both operating policies place the single site at L02 (busiest cell 2, just south of Herald Square); the deck heuristic's L01 (Penn Station cell) is equivalent for the ILP (+0.16 USD) and 4 USD better than L02 at equal size for the policy, so L01 vs L02 is a tie under both policies. The ordering of the multi-site deck layouts is also the same under both policies: the deck's 2-4 site layouts save the ILP 0.9-1.6 USD and the policy 48-67 USD of operations relative to `L02:SC:B3:C2` (but only 0.5-19 USD relative to `L02:SC:B4:C2`), against 13-40 USD of extra setup, so the policy is roughly break-even on two or three sites (regret 7.4-12.5 USD, within the seed noise above) whereas the ILP rejects every additional site.
* Size: not robust. The coordinator is indifferent between 3 and 4 spares at the one site (0.15 USD), the policy values the fourth spare at 48 USD of operating cost per shift (5 % of its operating cost) because it swaps without look-ahead and runs the site dry (stock-out slots 13.4 at B3 vs 7.4 at B4); both agree that two spares are too few (+8.3 USD for the ILP, +150.6 USD for the policy). A planner who expects the fleet to be run by a myopic policy should therefore buy the extra spare (450 USD of investment) even though the coordinator's value function says it is worthless.

## Anomalies and caveats

1. **`L02:SC:B2:C1` is not the exhaustive optimum of the full problem.** Its `sources` tag (`exhaustive@m1.0`, `integrated@m1.0`) comes from `--validate` (E9.1), which ran on a restricted protocol: 4 candidates (L01-L04), 2 seeds, menu `B in {1, 2}` only, `all_candidates` libraries. On the full menu and five seeds it is the worst of the seven layouts under both policies (ILP +8.3 USD, policy +150.6 USD). The sweep's own integrated run at m = 1 hit its 600 s limit with no certified bound (`gap inf`, incumbent `L02:SC:B3:C2`), so there is no certified bi-level optimum at m = 1; the agreement test (A) = (B) in E9.1 holds only for the restricted protocol.
2. **Four TIME_LIMIT solves, all on `L02:SC:B2:C1`**: its written V is an upper bound; with gaps 1.46 % (seed 0, V_0 = 705.2) and 0.35 % (seed 2) the true V could be at most 0.2 x (10.3 + 2.3) = 2.5 USD lower, which does not change any ranking (it stays >= 5.6 USD behind the B >= 3 layouts). Two spares at one site make the written end-target constraints bind, which is also why its price of the recovery targets (1.38 USD) is ten times that of the other layouts (0.02-0.42 USD).
3. **The ILP's service outcome is layout-independent on this family.** Served share 0.856 (30.8 of 36 orders), 1.8 late orders, 7.2 USD lateness, 465 USD response are identical for all six layouts with B >= 3 and on every seed; siting and sizing move only travel (11.8-12.4 USD) and electricity (0-0.8 USD). `nyc4h_stress2` therefore makes station siting bind for the myopic policy but still not for the coordinator: for the ILP the fleet of 6 bikes, not the batteries, is the binding resource, and a single SC site with 3 spares anywhere in the core is sufficient. The deck's multi-site layouts cannot pay for themselves under the ILP (0.9-1.6 USD operating saving vs 13.4-40.2 USD extra setup).
4. **Baseline in/out-of-sample gap is large and negative (74-128 USD)**, driven by seed heterogeneity rather than over-fitting (the search never optimised the baseline value); with five seeds the policy's layout ranking among `L02:SC:B4:C2` and the 2-4 site deck layouts (7-27 USD apart, seed range ~700 USD) is not statistically resolved, while the 150 USD penalty of the two-spare layout and the 43-47 USD penalty of three spares vs four are consistent on every seed (5 of 5 and 4 of 5 seeds respectively).
5. **`regret_baseline` in `RESULTS.md` is relative to the best of these seven layouts only**, not to the policy's optimum over the whole menu (no search was run with the baseline as objective); `L02:SC:B4:C2` happens to be the best of the seven for the policy because the greedy + LS at m >= 2 could afford the fourth spare.
6. **The run's `cross_eval.png` uses a single colour scale** across the ILP (654-693) and baseline (979-1129) columns, which hides every difference within the ILP column; `cross_eval_final.png` below normalises each policy column separately and adds the regret matrix.
7. Minor: the deck's m = 1 layout reports V = 655.06 and baseline V = 1072.3 on the three search seeds (`deck/RESULTS.md`) vs 654.65 and 1022.3 here on five seeds; the ILP number is consistent (in-sample OPEX 641.87 + 13.19), the baseline number drops because seeds 3-4 are easier for the policy. The 4-site deck layout has a higher baseline OPEX than the 3-site one (952.7 vs 946.3 USD) although it has strictly more capacity: policy noise, not a capacity effect.

## Interpretation

The cross-evaluation answers the E9.5 question (is the siting robust to the operating policy?) with a split verdict: the location is, the size is not. Every search method converged on the same place, the busiest restaurant cell L02, and the policy agrees; the Penn Station cell L01, which the deck heuristic picks because it has the most restaurants, is a statistical tie under both policies, so on this box the coordinator does not care where in the core the single site stands, and the policy cares only within 4 USD. What the two policies disagree about is how much to buy: the coordinator, which plans swaps with full look-ahead, extracts the same service (30.8 of 36 orders, 1.8 late) from three spares at one site as from fifteen spares at four sites, so every additional spare or site is pure setup cost to it and the bi-level optimum is the cheapest single SC site with three spares; the myopic policy runs the site dry (13 stock-out slots per shift at B = 3) and values the fourth spare at 48 USD per shift and the deck's additional sites at a further 0.5-19 USD, against 13-40 USD of setup. The practical reading is that the extra 450 USD for a fourth spare is insurance against the operator not being the coordinator, costs the coordinator 0.15 USD per shift, and should be bought; extra sites are break-even at best for the policy and never for the coordinator, so the first-site-only answer of the sweep stands under both policies. The size of the ILP-vs-policy gap, 31-41 % of the policy's cost and 306-467 USD per shift, is the same order as in E8 and now comes mostly from lateness: with scarce energy the policy's late deliveries (15-18 per shift) rather than its unserved orders dominate, and more stations reduce that gap from 36 to 31 % without closing it, which confirms the E8 asymmetry that the coordinator absorbs siting and the policy does not. The in-sample / out-of-sample check passes for the quantity the search optimised: the ILP OPEX moves by at most 1.0 USD between the search seeds and the held-out seeds, below the 8-13 USD steps that separate the layout classes, so the three-seed search did not over-fit; the policy's 75-128 USD swing between the two subsets is seed heterogeneity and warns that policy-side rankings closer than about 30 USD are not resolved with five seeds. Two caveats limit the claim: the restricted-menu validation layout is not a certified optimum and should not be quoted as the exhaustive answer, and the stressed family still fails to make the battery side bind for the coordinator, so a siting study whose planner value is the ILP needs either fewer spares per site (B = 2 is the only size at which the ILP reacts, by 8 USD) or more bikes per site before location and size carry information at the upper level. For the paper, the usable statement is therefore: on the stressed Midtown family the bi-level optimum is a single co-located site at the busiest restaurant cell, its location is invariant to the operating policy, its size should be set for the policy that will actually run the fleet, and the coordinator's advantage over the myopic policy (31-41 %) is not a substitute for, and is only weakly affected by, infrastructure.
