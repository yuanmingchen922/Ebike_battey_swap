# Round 6, transfers stage: value of battery transfers and hub separation at the new prices, final report

Source: `records.jsonl` in this directory (390 records, last record per key; no duplicate keys), compared with the legacy-price run `outputs/r5_x2_transfers/records.jsonl` (390 records) on byte-identical instances (demand md5 equal per seed in both runs). Every table and figure was recomputed from the records by `figs/make_r6_transfers_final.py`; this file was composed by `figs/write_results_final.py`. No ILP was re-solved. The auto-generated `RESULTS.md` / `RESULTS_x2.md` were used only as a cross-check (they agree with the recomputation where they overlap).

**Design.** Round-5 transfer design (X2) at the new prices P 60 USD per unserved order, w 18 USD/h from release, v 15 USD/h from the promise (cost variant `usd_r6`), family usd4h at nominal demand (6 bikes, 36 orders, 48 five-minute slots, eBaaS energy calibration), seeds 0-4. Three layouts: co-located (3 swap-and-charge sites, 3 spares and 2 chargers each); separated, 1 hub (3 swap-only sites with 3 spares, one charge-only hub with 6 chargers); separated, 2 hubs (two hubs with 3 chargers each). Two timetables: direct shuttles every 30 min at 20 USD per run; cyclic tours, one per hour, with the 20 USD fare split over the legs (6.67 / 5.00 / 4.00 USD per leg for 3 / 4 / 5 facilities). Transfer switch: forbidden (`none`, solved once per instance on the shuttle library, compared with the shuttle-timetable rules) or allowed (`free`). Targets: relaxed (rho 0, R_end 0; the headline) and written (rho 0.5, R_end 1 at co-located sites, 0 at swap-only sites and hubs; the price of recovery). Aggregated ILP, Gurobi Threads 3, Seed 0, TimeLimit 300 s, libraries price-checked (108 arcs per context). Rules: r2, the reorder-point transfer rule, M2, M3, M2+M3; best rule = cheapest of r2, M2, M3, M2+M3 on the instance and timetable (end-adjusted total for the end-adjusted gap, raw total for the raw gap). Gap = 100 (best rule - ILP) / best rule per instance, then averaged. Means with exact Student-t 95 % CIs in brackets; every difference is paired by seed; n 5 per cell unless stated. `[exact]` marks a paired difference that is identical on all seeds.

## 1. Problems and anomalies (read first)

1. **16 of 90 ILP solves stopped at the 300 s limit and this stage was not retried** (the retry pass covered the headline, price-sweep, lateness-sweep, fleet and levers stages only). The largest MIP gap is 2.89 %. **One** of the 16 is certified optimal by the nesting argument ILP(transfers allowed) <= ILP(transfers forbidden) on the same instance (Appendix B): the relaxed no-transfer solve with one hub on seed 3 (0.29 %), whose incumbent equals an optimal transfers-allowed value. 15 remain open. Two of them are transfers-allowed solves that stopped on their MIP start, the transfer-free optimum, without improving it: relaxed, 2 hubs, direct shuttles, seed 3 (0.60 %; optimum between 667.40 and 671.46 USD) and written, co-located, cyclic tours, seed 3 (0.51 %; between 610.21 and 613.33 USD). Nesting bounds such a row only from above, so neither is certified (the first version of this report marked both certified; see 'Verification corrections'). The other 13 are written-target cells in the separated layouts: 8 with transfers on shuttles (2.13-2.89 %), 3 with transfers on cyclic tours (0.47-0.98 %) and 2 **without transfers on seed 1** (1 hub 1.44 %, 2 hubs 0.88 %). The two seed-1 no-transfer cells had no accepted MIP start (the r2 start is infeasible under the written targets), so on that seed the value of transfers and the price of recovery rest on incumbents; the report therefore gives the provable bracket next to every incumbent difference (for example, the value of cyclic transfers with one hub is 63.87 USD on incumbents and lies between 61.73 and 65.83 USD by the bounds; the no-transfer price of recovery with one hub is 71.88 USD on incumbents and lies between 69.75 and 71.88 USD). None of the conclusions depends on the open gaps: transfers are provably worth more than zero on all 5 seeds in all four separated written cells, and under relaxed targets the value of transfers is proven zero on 29 of the 30 instance-timetable pairs and at most 4.06 USD on the open one.
2. **The operating part of the separation cost is not exactly zero under relaxed targets.** With two hubs the separated optimum is 1.90 (seed 1) and 1.14 USD (seed 2) cheaper in operations than the co-located optimum (mean -0.62 USD, CI -1.70 to 0.45; response time -1.5 USD, travel -0.36 USD), which a pure restriction could not produce. The hub locations enter the road graph as nodes with routes: the arc builder creates wait and move arcs over every node and route and swap and terminal arcs over swap facilities only, and the library grows from 2.14 M arcs co-located to 2.36 M with one hub and 2.58 M with two hubs on seed 1, so a bike can wait at or route through a hub node. The separated layouts are therefore not restrictions of the co-located one, and 'separation costs exactly the fixed charge' must be read as 'within about 1 USD of the fixed charge' (29.20 against 29.19 USD with one hub; 57.76 against 58.38 USD with two). The legacy run had the same effect (-0.19 / -0.34 USD) without noting the cause.
3. **The relaxed optima no longer buy zero energy.** All 45 relaxed solves move no battery, but the three co-located solves of seed 1 (no transfers, shuttles, cyclic) buy 0.139 kWh (0.044 USD); 42 of 45 solves (14 of 15 instances) buy nothing. The manuscript's 'bought no energy and moved no battery' becomes 'moved no battery and, on one instance, bought 0.14 kWh'.
4. **The best rule is r2 on every one of the 60 instance-timetable-target cells at the new prices** (M2 on none; at the legacy prices M2 was cheapest on 7 of 15 instances, 28 of 60 cells). M2 and r2 schedules are physically identical to the legacy ones (they do not read prices); M2 drops more orders on seeds 2 and 3 (6 and 5 against 4) and its lower lateness on the other seeds is worth less at v 15. The per-instance selection of the benchmark therefore buys nothing here, and the 'best rule' columns equal the r2 columns.
5. **M3 reads the unserved-order price and got worse.** M3 and M2+M3 schedules changed on 31 and 28 of 60 cells relative to the legacy run (r2, the reorder-point rule and M2 on none). At P 60 M3 ships more often (2.6-3.4 services per window on shuttles against 1.8-3.2 at the legacy prices) and its loss against r2 grew: co-located shuttles 88.96 USD against 50.60 (paired +38.36, CI 7.62-69.11, dearer on 5 of 5 seeds). It no longer halves the reorder-point loss on shuttles (it removes 28-39 % of it) and it is dearer than the reorder-point rule on cyclic tours in both separated layouts (+48.61, CI 9.53-87.68, and +41.28, CI -19.78 to 102.35).
6. **The cyclic-tour advantage of the ILP is a fare convention, not a routing benefit, and this is now exact.** With transfers allowed the written-target ILP is 14.62 (1 hub) and 15.87 USD (2 hubs) cheaper on cyclic tours than on shuttles; the same 2.0-2.2 batteries are moved in one service on both timetables, and the transfer charges differ by 15.0 and 15.8 USD (22.2 / 22.0 USD for a shuttle carrying two batteries against 7.2 / 6.2 USD for one cyclic leg). The provable part (cyclic incumbent minus shuttle bound) is -2.49 (CI -11.69 to 6.71) and -3.67 USD (-12.86 to 5.53); the cyclic incumbent lies below the shuttle bound on 4 of 5 seeds in each layout.
7. **Written-target rule schedules violate the end targets.** 148 of 150 written-target rule rows fail the reported-only end-state checks (18) (and (17) on 32 of them); only the reorder-point rule on shuttles passes on seed 2 in both separated layouts. All 150 pass modulo the reported-only families and all 150 relaxed rows pass. The rules are identical across target settings except M3 and M2+M3 in the co-located layout (they read the written reserve). Written-target gaps therefore compare an ILP that meets the targets with rules that mostly do not and understate the ILP advantage.
8. **Wide absolute CIs; paired CIs carry the weight, except for the value of transfers.** Seed-to-seed variation in rule cost is large (best-rule CI half-width about 157 USD at n 5), so per-layout gap CIs span about 20 points. The value of transfers under written targets varies from 26 to 108 USD across seeds (CI half-width 43 USD), because it is one to two unserved orders at 60 USD each; its sign is nevertheless certain on every seed.
9. **Harder solves at the new prices.** 16 time-limited solves against 11 in the legacy run, mean solver time 125 s against 85 s, stage wall time 3.46 h (12,467 s, 2026-10-19 23:13 to 2026-10-20 02:40 on the laptop) against 2.31 h; the limit used was 300 s on every row, none was shortened by the wall budget.
10. **Twelve MIP starts were rejected** (written targets, no transfers: co-located seeds 0 and 3, both separated layouts seeds 0-4), because the r2 start violates the end targets (verification status INFEASIBLE); the same twelve cells were rejected in the legacy run. Ten of the twelve solves still reached optimality; the two seed-1 cells are the open ones of item 1.
11. **Even without end targets the ILP drops one order on seeds 2 and 3** (served share 0.972; 0.4 unserved orders per window on average) at P 60; at the legacy prices it dropped two orders on seeds 1-3 (0.944). This is the price-dependence of service already documented in the headline stage, not a transfer effect.

## 3. Results at the new prices

### 3.1 Relaxed targets (headline): transfers are worth nothing, separation costs the fixed charge

In all 45 relaxed solves (n 5 seeds x 3 layouts x {no transfers, shuttles, cyclic tours}; 43 optimal, 2 at the 300 s time limit) the ILP uses no transfer service and moves no battery, and the transfers-allowed incumbent equals the transfer-free value on every instance (paired difference 0.00 USD, exact, on every seed, both timetables, all layouts). Equality of the optima is proven on 29 of the 30 instance-timetable pairs; the time-limited no-transfer solve with one hub on seed 3 (0.29 %) is certified by nesting. On 2 hubs, direct shuttles, seed 3 the transfers-allowed solve stopped at 0.60 % without improving its MIP start, the transfer-free optimum of 671.46 USD; its bound of 667.40 USD leaves up to 4.06 USD of value of transfers open on that instance (0.83 USD on the cell mean, Table 3.2), so that row is not certified. Every other bound-based upper limit is at most 0.06 USD per instance (MIP tolerance). 42 of 45 solves buy no energy; the three co-located solves of seed 1 buy 0.139 kWh (0.044 USD). The gap to the best rule (r2 on every instance) is 24.98 % (14.04-35.92) co-located, 24.10 % (13.40-34.79) with one hub and 23.36 % (12.87-33.86) with two hubs (end-adjusted, n 5 each); pooled over the 15 layout-seed cells 24.15 % (19.71-28.59), and with the five seed means as units 24.15 % (13.44-34.86) end-adjusted or 24.21 % (13.50-34.92) raw. Separating chargers from swap sites costs 29.20 USD per window (29.15-29.24) with one hub against a fixed charge of 29.19 USD, and 57.76 USD (56.68-58.83) with two hubs against 58.38 USD (operating part +0.01 and -0.62 USD; anomaly 2). The best rule pays 29.21 and 58.40 USD.

### 3.2 Written targets: transfers are worth 49-65 USD per window in the separated layouts, nothing co-located

With the written targets the swap-only sites must host every swap from their initial three spares (R_end 0, no charger), while rho 0.5 forces late swaps; without transfers the coordinator drops 1.6 (1 hub) and 1.8 (2 hubs) orders per window instead of 0.4, and the served share falls from 0.989 to 0.956 / 0.950. Transfers restore the relaxed service (0.989) with one service carrying 2.0-2.2 batteries. Their value on incumbents is 63.87 USD (20.79-106.94) with one hub and 64.64 USD (21.18-108.11) with two on cyclic tours, 9.08 % (3.78-14.37) and 8.82 % (3.64-14.00) of the ILP cost without transfers; on shuttles the incumbents give 49.24 USD (6.34-92.14) and 48.78 USD (5.15-92.41), 6.91 % and 6.57 %. The provable brackets from the solver bounds are 61.73-65.83 and 63.30-65.28 USD (cyclic) and 47.11-61.38 and 47.43-60.98 USD (shuttles); the value is provably positive on 5 of 5 seeds in all four cells. Solve status of the one-hub cyclic cell (n 5, paired): no transfers 4 optimal and 1 time limit (seed 1, 1.44 %), cyclic transfers 3 optimal and 2 time limit (seeds 3 and 4, 0.98 and 0.59 %), 300 s each, not retried; per seed the value is 26.47 / 108.20 / 55.26 / 90.92 / 38.49 USD on incumbents and 26.47 / 97.55-108.20 / 55.26 / 90.88-97.27 / 38.49-41.94 USD by the bounds, so the smallest provable value is 26.47 USD (seed 0, both solves optimal). The status of every cell is in the last column of Table 3.2. Almost all of the value is unserved orders avoided: 72.00 USD (9.67-134.33) with one hub and 84.00 USD (-0.94 to 168.94) with two, that is 1.2 and 1.4 orders at 60 USD, against -4.5 / -13.8 USD of response time (the no-transfer optimum that drops orders answers the rest faster), +5.0 / +2.25 USD of lateness, and -7.2 / -6.2 USD of transfer charges on cyclic tours (-22.2 / -22.0 USD on shuttles). ## 5. Interpretation

**Shown by the records.**

- Without end-of-window targets, transfers are worth nothing to the coordinator on usd4h: no battery is moved in any of the 45 relaxed solves, and the transfers-allowed incumbent equals the transfer-free value on all 30 instance-timetable pairs at either price; this is proven on 30 of 30 pairs at the legacy prices and on 29 of 30 at the new prices (one time-limited 2-hub shuttle solve leaves at most 4.06 USD open). Separating chargers from swap sites is then a fixed-charge decision (29.20 / 57.76 USD against charges of 29.19 / 58.38 USD).
- Under enforced recovery in a separated layout, transfers are worth about 64 USD per window on cyclic tours and about 49 USD on shuttles (incumbents; at least 47 USD by the bounds), 7-9 % of the ILP cost, provably positive on every seed. This value is service: 1.2-1.4 unserved orders avoided per window at 60 USD each; the transfer charges (6-22 USD) and electricity (0.3 USD) are small against it.
- Transfers cut the price of recovery in the separated layouts from about 72 USD to 7-8 USD (cyclic) or 23 USD (shuttles), but separation still costs 6-8 USD more than its fixed charge in the best case and 71 USD more without transfers; it does not pay in any setting.
- The ILP's cyclic-tour advantage (14.6-15.9 USD) is the fare split; the physical plans coincide.
- No rule-based transfer policy is ever strictly cheaper than r2 (0 of 60 cells for each rule, as at the legacy prices); M3 ships more and loses more at P 60.
- The change from the legacy prices is a repricing of the same mechanism: the value of transfers and the no-transfer price of recovery rise by the same 47-48 USD on incumbents (45-49 USD on cyclic tours and without transfers by the solver bounds), and every price-free quantity (relaxed separation cost, price of recovery with transfers, cyclic-versus-shuttle contrast, reorder-point loss in separated layouts) is unchanged within 2 USD on incumbents, with paired CIs that include 0. By the bounds the price of recovery with cyclic transfers changed by between -1.82 and +0.14 USD (one hub); the cyclic-versus-shuttle contrast is not resolved by the bounds.

**Suggested, not shown.**

- That the value of transfers scales linearly with P: only two price points on five seeds, and the per-seed value ranges from 26 to 108 USD because it counts whole orders. A statement of the form 'about one order per window' is safer than a USD figure.
- That transfers would be first-order where batteries bind: not tested (no stressed family in this design); on usd4h they stay second-order (9 % of ILP cost, and only under enforced recovery in separated layouts).
- That cyclic tours are operationally better than shuttles: the ILP contrast is a pricing convention; the rule contrast (91-106 USD for the reorder-point rule) reflects fewer services, not better routing.
- The written-target gaps (15-25 %) as a like-for-like comparison: the rules do not meet the targets.
- The headline gap of this experiment (24.2 %) as an independent estimate: the five co-located instances are usd4h seeds 0-4 of the headline stage, and the three layouts share demand per seed.

## Appendix B. Time-limited ILP rows and their certification by nesting

ILP(transfers allowed) <= ILP(transfers forbidden) on the same instance and targets, because the transfer-free schedule is feasible with transfers allowed. A time-limited transfers-free row whose incumbent equals an optimal transfers-allowed value is therefore optimal. The converse does not hold: for a time-limited transfers-allowed row whose incumbent equals the optimal transfers-free value (its MIP start), nesting gives only ILP(free) <= ILP(none), an upper limit that the incumbent already meets, so the row stays open between its bound and its incumbent. The first version of this appendix used the inequality in the wrong direction and marked two such rows (relaxed, 2 hubs, shuttles, seed 3; written, co-located, cyclic tours, seed 3) certified; 1 of the 16 rows is certified.
